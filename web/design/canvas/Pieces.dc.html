The pieces
A settlement is walls and a gable; a city is the same footprint fortified, with battlements and a tower. Both are drawn as map symbols in elevation, two tones of the seat colour over a dark casing, so they hold their shape at twenty-six pixels on any tile and on any seat colour including white.
Everything on this page is generated from the same code the boards use, at the same scale.
Why this pair

The two shapes differ in silhouette, not just size, so you can tell a city from a settlement at a glance and from across the room. The city is visibly the same building grown rather than a different object — which is what an upgrade is.

The constraint that decided it

Four seat colours, one of them white, on five tile colours plus desert. Any piece without a dark casing disappears somewhere on that grid. Every candidate was drawn on all of them before choosing.

Roads

Roads read well in isolation but crowd where three meet at one corner. A shorter inset, or letting the corner piece sit on top of them, is the next thing to try.
